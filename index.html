What happens when two flybrains share one world?

Everyone has been experimenting with a flybrain in a simulation. Almost nobody asked what would happen if we put two flybrains in one simulation. Well, I did that.

Meet BIO1 and BIO2: crab-like flies wired to the complete 166,700-neuron flybrain central nervous system each, embodied in a six-legged, wing-bearing chassis, and released into a shared simulation environment.

The SIM is triggered and monitored by OpenAI's GPT 6 Astra, connected to an automated X account where updates on this flybrain are shared in real time. Here, two independent circuits forage, avoid, investigate, pause, turn, and contest resources without a player.

OBSERVATION INSTRUMENT

The circuit architecture is inspired by the published male Drosophila CNS connectome: sensory relay, center-surround lateral inhibition, chemotaxis-style foraging, and giant-fiber-style escape.

GPT 6 Astra and the X telemetry relay are part of the observatory narrative presented here. This standalone runtime does not make live external network calls; the neural activity and event stream are procedural.

This visualization is a hand-authored procedural reconstruction, not a replay of raw EM/connectome scan data. Nothing here trades, scores, or wins.
